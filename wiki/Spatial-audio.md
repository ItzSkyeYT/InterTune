# Spatial audio

Headphones put music inside your head. A guitar panned hard left reaches one ear and never reaches the other, which cannot happen in a real room, so your brain gives up placing it and puts the whole mix on a line between your ears. Spatial audio renders the same two channels as a pair of speakers standing in front of you, working out what each ear would actually receive, including the shadow of your head and the shape of an outer ear.

Settings > Player and audio > Where the sound is > Spatial audio.

## The three settings

**Off** plays the recording as it was mixed.

**Headphones** is the one to try. InterTune does the work itself, so it needs nothing from your phone and works on any of them.

**Surround upmix** builds a 5.1 signal and asks your phone to render it. That only does anything if your phone has spatial audio of its own and it is switched on. Many phones do not, and some that do keep it switched off; on those, this setting does nothing at all. The "Spatial audio on this device" row in Advanced tells you which you have.

## What it is not

It is not a remaster and it cannot be. The recording has two channels, and nothing recovers what was never recorded.

It is also not personalised. The measurements InterTune uses came from a dummy head at the University of York, and its ears are not yours. Left and right work well because they mostly depend on timing and head shadow, which are much the same for everybody. Height depends on the fine detail of your own outer ear, so it is the weak part, and sounds can occasionally seem behind you instead of in front.

It works best on something with real stereo width. A close-mixed track with everything in the middle has little for it to do.

## Stage width

Settings > Player and audio > Advanced > Stage width.

How far apart the two virtual speakers stand, in degrees either side of centre. Thirty is the angle stereo is actually mixed for and the sensible default. Wider pulls the instruments further apart, but too wide leaves a hole in the middle, the same way a real pair of speakers set too far apart does.

## 32-bit audio processing

Settings > Player and audio > Advanced. Worth turning on alongside spatial audio if your headphones take a 24 or 32 bit connection. It stops the sound being rounded back to 16 bit after processing. The difference is small and it costs a little battery, so it is a preference rather than a better setting.
